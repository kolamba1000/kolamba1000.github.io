Task

Help plan production

Every day, a store manager decides how much to bake and how much to order. An analyst compares real sales with the forecast and looks for model errors. One interface for everyone

  • Store manager

    Decides how much to bake and order every day. Right now, they rely on experience and yesterday’s leftovers

    Pain point: guesses by eye, wastes time

  • Analyst

    Compares real sales with the forecast and looks for model errors

    Pain point: checks everything by hand in spreadsheets

Limits

Limits of the design

  • ID, no name

    A store shows up as an ID, like 12345. There is no store name in the interface

  • Direction only

    The gap between real sales and the forecast is a number and a color. The design decides the direction

  • Different detail levels

    The forecast is by day. Real sales are by week. The design joins them in one table

  • No formula

    The quality score from 1 to 10 is shown as is, with no formula behind it

Process

From benchmarks to handoff

First, research and benchmarks. Then visual style, a design system, screens, and copy. At the end, two rounds of review. Each step built on the one before it, and review sent work back a step when needed. Result: 125 variables and 67 components in the design system, 243 frames in three sizes

Solution

One table instead of 390 rows

15 products times 26 stores makes 390 rows, if every pair gets its own row.
I did it another way. Each row is one product: the total across all stores. Click a row to see each store. No number gets lost

  • 390 rows, if every pair gets its own row
  • 26 stores open with one click

Color should not confuse

A forecast miss is bad both ways: too little stock or too much waste. Red and green read as ‘bad’ and ‘good’

Solution

Top 5 deviations, not all 26

The infographic could list all 26 stores. Instead, it shows only five:
the ones with the biggest gap from the forecast. The design already points to where the problem is

Solution

The design names the filter

Saving a filter usually means typing a name for it. Here the name builds itself: city, store, and product. All that’s left is to click ‘Save’

  • 3 parts in the name: city, store, product

Handoff

What goes to developers

  • 24 outline icon assets
  • 10 open questions
  • Components, variables, and styles are named just like in Figma
  • The chart is not a component. It’s a spec: axes, colors, number of series
  • The quality score formula is unknown. This is an open question

Top 3 questions:

  • Full list of stores and cities
  • Does the data have a ‘kg’ unit for items sold by weight?
  • Formula and threshold for ‘Deviation from average’
